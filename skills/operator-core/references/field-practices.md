# Field Practices — Operator Core

Actionable moves organized by chapter. Each practice is a verb, not a vibe.

## Chapter 1: Thinking & Cognition
- Schedule ten minutes of undistracted reflection on your hardest problem before opening any inbox.
- Name the lock when you catch yourself dismissing an idea unexamined: bias, habit, ego, or noise.
- Run one lock audit monthly: what am I protecting, what am I avoiding?
- Write down one belief you hold about your work and the evidence that would change it — then go look.
- Retire one model per quarter that reality has already voted against.
- Run a weekly review: what happened, what did I notice, what did I miss?
- Begin each day with ten quiet minutes aiming your attention before the feed aims it.
- After any notable event, write the lesson it was carrying in one sentence.

## Chapter 2: Problem Solving
- Before acting on any alarming report, run one independent verification proportional to stakes.
- Ask for the requirement once, in writing, then stop interrogating and start executing.
- Label your next three problems clear, complicated, complex, or chaotic.
- For your current problem, write the symptom, then ask "what caused that?" four more times.
- Force three genuinely different options onto one page before your next decision.
- Collect input before revealing your own position.
- Weigh costs, second-order effects, and who bears the risk — same scale for every option.
- Take every precaution first, then write: "Camel tied. Outcome released."

## Chapter 3: Decision Making
- Before your next significant decision, set a consultation window with an end date.
- Gather dissent before declaring your lean; positions harden once spoken.
- Write down what you want to be true, then deliberately weigh the case against it.
- Design one low-cost, reversible probe for your biggest open commitment.
- After every decided matter, mark it "closed" — ban re-litigation without new evidence.

## Chapter 4: Pressure & Hardship
- When anxiety spikes, write two columns — fear (future) and grief (past) — then one line: the right thing for the next hour.
- Pair every grind with a daily reset ritual: ten minutes of silence, reflection, or counsel.
- In your next overwhelm, renegotiate one scope item instead of absorbing it silently.
- Before high-stakes moments, run a calm protocol: breathe, review preparation, state the ground of confidence.
- After finishing any hard effort, stand up for the next one within twenty-four hours: finish, rise, aim.
- Before the hard conversation, make the three asks: capacity, ease, clear speech.

## Chapter 5: Adaptability
- Name your current mission in one sentence — then list which headings you've been treating as missions.
- Pick one external condition you're waiting on, replace with one internal change you control.
- Plot your last two years as cycles, not events — write what the last downturn revealed.
- Break your biggest pending change into three staged installs with consolidation time.
- Identify one hostile environment you're trying to win that you should simply outlast.

## Chapter 6: Innovation
- Name the monolith your new thing must break from — and split cleanly.
- Identify your single sustaining resource before launch; secure it for the longest runway.
- Commit to the best available direction for ninety days before demanding certainty.
- Write every idea within the hour; unwritten ideas are donations to the void.
- Name the failure in one sentence, without adjectives, then close the file.

## Chapter 7: Execution
- Define "done" as "better than asked," then measure the delta before shipping.
- Write acceptance criteria before starting any task over one hour.
- Replace one progress meeting per week with a shipped artifact.
- Stake your week's self-respect on shipped output, not hours logged.
- Finish the small tail of every task: the follow-up note, the renamed file, the closed loop.

## Chapter 8: Planning
- Forecast your lean years: the downturn, gap, or dry season you can see coming.
- Save in the fat months by rule, not by mood — a fixed percentage, automatically.
- Map your single points of failure: one client, one income, one supplier, one key person.
- Maintain a readiness budget: hours and dollars spent on capabilities you hope not to need.
- Add "assuming no blockers" to every commitment — then list the likely blockers.

## Chapter 9: Discipline
- Audit yesterday's hours like money: which were spent, which invested, which stolen?
- Defend sleep as a scheduled system reset, not negotiable downtime.
- Reserve one daily unreachable block for your hardest cognitive work.
- Run periodic restraint drills: a digital fast, a spending freeze, a comfort skipped.

## Chapter 10: Wealth
- Move idle balances into productive soil: assets, people, capability.
- Send a written recap after every agreement, same day.
- Set your burn rate between the floor of stinginess and the ceiling of depletion.
- Tie giving to income by rule — a percentage, not a mood.
- Maintain three or more provision channels before you need them.

## Chapter 11: Leadership
- Open every major decision with a fixed window for structured consultation.
- Audit your tone when results dip; harshness is an attrition tax you pay in silence.
- Inventory your current trusts: role, budget, data, relationships, confidential knowledge.
- Score every candidate separately on competence and integrity; reject any single-axis hire.

## Chapter 12: Conflict
- Classify incoming hostility, then deliberately answer one level above it.
- In any dispute, price both options: retaliation costs vs. pardon unlocks.
- When you hold the leverage, open with amnesty, not with the invoice.
- Route deadlocks to the shared principle, never to seniority or volume.

## Chapter 13: Communication
- Replace softening euphemism with direct, checkable statements.
- Match content, timing, and depth to the actual listener.
- Label your speech: verified, inference, or speculation — say which before you speak.
- Default to neutral interpretations until evidence upgrades them.
- Budget attention by teachability, not by rank.

## Chapter 14: Failure
- Time your recovery latency: how long from mistake to admitted, repaired, stopped?
- Admit significant errors within the hour, before anyone asks.
- Schedule recurring self-audits; integrity is maintained, never achieved.
- Open every post-mortem with "what in our own system caused this?"
- End every failure process with a restored mission, not a reduced identity.

## Chapter 15: Success
- After each major win, list what you controlled versus what you rode — in writing.
- Install a win ritual: within 24 hours, credit sources and convert gain into giving.
- Pair every celebration with a self-audit session — same week, non-negotiable.
- Cap both the celebration spike and the despair dip with pre-set rituals.
- Stress-test your success narrative: who and what else built this garden?
